Abstract

LaborChain is an ontological framework for modeling and tracing labor exploitation conditions embedded in AI training lineages. Building on prior work introducing siliconwashing — the use of progressive ethics discourse to obscure material harms inflicted on workers in AI production — LaborChain provides a conceptual taxonomy of exploitation types, a vocabulary of entities and relations for representing labor conditions in training supply chains, and an inheritance model for tracing how exploitation provenance propagates when one model is fine-tuned from another. Just as bias propagates through model lineages, so does the record of exploitative labor conditions underlying base models, and unlike bias, it does not dissolve with downstream fine-tuning. The framework is validated against two independently documented cases — the OpenAI/Sama case and the Scale AI/Remotasks case — using exclusively third-party journalistic and worker testimony sources, demonstrating that LaborChain does not depend on company self-disclosure. Unpopulated fields are themselves legible as evidence of siliconwashing. LaborChain provides infrastructure for future researchers and practitioners to build on, extending model cards and datasheets into the domain of production labor.

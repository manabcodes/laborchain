Introduction back to ToC

AI systems marketed as ethical and human-centered depend on a hidden production infrastructure: workers in peripheral states who label data, moderate traumatic content, and provide reinforcement learning from human feedback under conditions of systematic exploitation. None of the existing documentation frameworks — Model Cards, Datasheets for Datasets, AI Cards — ask who labeled the data, under what conditions, at what wage, in what jurisdiction, or whether workers could refuse. LaborChain is designed to fill this gap. It makes five contributions: it operationalizes siliconwashing into a formal ontological structure; it introduces labor exploitation provenance as a tractable concept analogous to data provenance in the fairness literature; it proposes an inheritance model in which exploitation provenance propagates through fine-tuning lineages in much the same way bias does; it validates the framework against two independently documented cases using exclusively third-party sources; and it deploys the framework as [LaborTrail](https://huggingface.co/spaces/sage-lollipop/laborchain), a publicly accessible web interface for generating and sharing LaborChain Model Cards. The formal OWL ontology and SPARQL validation suite are publicly available at [https://anonymous.4open.science/r/laborchain56C3/](https://anonymous.4open.science/r/laborchain56C3/). This document provides the ontology specification. The accompanying paper is submitted to AIES 2026.
